✂ Clipper

Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 20, 2026

Clipper is a personal tool used by its owner only, on the owner's own computer. This policy explains what Clipper accesses when the owner connects their social media accounts, and what it does with it.

What Clipper accesses

  • TikTok: basic profile information (username and display name) to show which account is connected, and permission to publish videos to that account (scopes user.info.basic and video.publish).
  • YouTube (Google): the connected channel's name, and permission to upload videos to that channel (scopes youtube.readonly and youtube.upload).
  • Instagram (Meta): the connected account's username and ID, and permission to publish Reels to that account.
  • Sign-in tokens issued by these platforms, so Clipper can upload when the owner asks it to.

Clipper does not read messages, contacts, followers, comments, analytics, or any other content from these accounts, and it does not access information about any other person.

How it's used

This information is used only to show which account is connected and to upload a clip when the owner approves it and clicks Upload. Nothing is posted automatically.

Where it's stored

Sign-in tokens and account names are stored only on the owner's own computer, in a file readable only by the owner's user account. Clipper has no server, database or cloud service of its own, and sends this information only to the platform it belongs to (for example, a TikTok token is only ever sent to TikTok).

Sharing

Clipper does not sell, share, rent or transfer any user data to anyone, and does not use it for advertising or to train AI models.

Google user data

Clipper's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Clipper uses YouTube API Services; see the YouTube Terms of Service and the Google Privacy Policy. You can revoke Clipper's access to your Google account at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Removing access and deleting data

Clicking Disconnect in Clipper deletes that platform's stored token. Access can also be revoked in each platform's own settings (TikTok: Settings → Security → Manage app permissions; Instagram: Settings → Apps and websites; Google: the link above). Clipper keeps no other copy.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the date above will be updated. Questions: streamingchaoshq@gmail.com.
